# Joint scalar and auxiliary extension by three new pole observations

This is an independently audited paper proof, using the earlier audited
boundary extension and body lemmas and an external exact finite base.
It is not a Lean theorem. Its conclusion concerns the actual zero-prefix
source subspace and its scalar/auxiliary image. It does **not** assert
independence of the entire specified equation-(24) family: the bridge
from this image statement to the remaining source-coordinate kernel is
a separate obligation.

The source conventions, fixed-transition base schemes, boundary scalar
extension, inherited trace, and body selectors are those of
[SOURCE24_ZERO_PREFIX_EXTENSION.md](SOURCE24_ZERO_PREFIX_EXTENSION.md),
especially (ZP26)--(ZP39) and (ZP61)--(ZP78). This note retains the same
formal source coefficients through both projections. It resolves the
joint-extension obligation stated after (ZP78).

## Statement and actual source conventions

Fix

\[
 G_*=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&2&1\\1&3&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_t=G_*\operatorname{diag}(1,u_t,v_t),\qquad
 L_{ta}=\sum_b A_t(a,b)X_b,
 \quad
 K_t=\operatorname{diag}(L_t)^{-1}A_t\operatorname{diag}(X).
 \tag{JE1}
\]

Write \(D_m=\prod_{t\le m,a}L_{ta}\).
The actual zero-prefix subspace \(\mathcal Z_m\) is generated by the
same-shared-index first-slot differences and the long-empty
differences in the preceding note. Its rows \(R\) have zero row sum,
component \(R_a\) divisible by \(X_a\) after clearing denominators, and
vanish at the coordinate vertices. Its defining useful property is
that the same formal coefficients append \(R\) to \(RK_n\).

Let \(n=q+1\), with old blocks \(1,\ldots,q\). Let \(P_s\) be the old
word with \(s\) first and the remaining blocks in ascending order, and
let \(Q^t\) be the ascending old word omitting \(t\). The new columns
used below have the exact expressions

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{t,n}^{ab}
   &=X_aK_t(a,b)e_b(Q^tK_n-K_nQ^t),\\
 C_{n,s}^{ab}
   &=X_aK_n(a,b)e_b(P_1-P_s),\\
 E_a
   &=F^{\rm empty}_{n,a}-F^{\rm empty}_{1,a}
     =X_ae_a(P_1K_n-K_nP_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{JE2}
\]

Thus all columns used are actual source differences with the prescribed
word order. No closure under arbitrary word permutations or
effect-dependent scalar multiplication is used.

Define the scalar and auxiliary polynomial maps

\[
 \mathcal A_m(R)=D_mR_2/X_2,\qquad
 \mathcal B_w(R)=
 -\frac1w\frac{(D_mR_1/X_1)|_{X_0=-wX_2}}{X_2},
 \qquad
 d_m=9\binom m2+3m.
 \tag{JE3}
\]

The divisions are removable. The second one in \(\mathcal B_w\)
uses \(R(e_1)=0\). The auxiliary target is the space of binary forms
of degree \(3m-1\), of dimension \(3m\).
On the scalar kernel, zero row sum and coordinate divisibility give
\(R_1=X_0X_1f\), and

\[
 \mathcal B_w(R)=(D_mf)|_{X_0=-wX_2}.
 \tag{JE4}
\]

The fixed-\(G_*\) scalar ambient has dimension \(d_m\), including the
nonreduced old-block conditions described in (ZP26)--(ZP34).

**Conditional joint extension.** For \(q\ge4\), suppose generically in
the fixed-\(G_*\) old parameters that

\[
 \operatorname{rank}\mathcal A_q=d_q,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal B_w|_{\ker\mathcal A_q})=3q.
 \tag{JE5}
\]

Then the same assertions hold generically at length \(n=q+1\), with
ranks \(d_n\) and \(3n\). Equivalently, the joint rank increases from
\(d_q+3q\) to \(d_n+3n\). The proof below is over constant real source
coefficients: coefficients may depend rationally on model parameters,
but never on \(X\).

The accepted exact length-four base gives scalar rank \(66\) and
auxiliary kernel rank \(12\). Consequently this conditional extension
proves generic joint ranks \(d_m+3m\) for every \(m\ge4\), subject to
that external exact base and the established paper lemmas cited here.

## A scalar projection regular at the merged-line boundary

Set \(u_n=\varepsilon,\ v_n=v\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_{n0}&=X_0+\varepsilon X_1+vX_2,&
 L_{n1}&=X_0+2\varepsilon X_1+vX_2,\\
 L_{n2}&=X_0+3\varepsilon X_1+2vX_2,&
 \ell&=X_0+vX_2,\quad \widetilde\ell=X_0+2vX_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{JE6}
\]

At \(\varepsilon=0\), the first two rows of \(K_n\) coincide. For
any zero-sum old row,
\[
 RK_n=\mu R_2(e_2-e_0),\qquad
 \mu=\frac{vX_0X_2}{\ell\widetilde\ell}.
\]
In particular every appended old row has component one identically
zero at the boundary, and an old scalar-kernel row appends to the
zero **vector**, not just to a zero scalar component.

Choose an old scalar basis. Let \(\mathscr B(\varepsilon)\) consist
of its appended columns and the following \(6q+3\) boundary columns:

\[
\begin{gathered}
 A_{ta}=C_{t,n}^{a0}\quad(t\le q,\ a=0,1),\qquad
 A_{*a}=\sum_bC_{1,n}^{ab}\quad(a=0,1),\\
 B_{sa}=C_{n,s}^{a2}\quad(2\le s\le q,\ a=1,2),\\
 S_t=\sum_bC_{t,n}^{2b},\qquad
 T_1=E_2-S_1,\quad T_2=E_2-S_2+B_{22},\\
 M_{t0}=C_{t,n}^{02},\quad M_{t2}=C_{t,n}^{20}\quad(t\le q),
 \qquad E_2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{JE7}
\]

The audited boundary theorem says that their scalar constant matrix
has rank \(d_q+6q+3=d_n-3q\), and spans the full actual boundary
scalar image.

Hypothesis (JE5), with auxiliary parameter \(v\), supplies \(3q\)
old scalar-kernel rows whose appended columns are denoted
\(\mathscr J(\varepsilon)\). For an old row
\(R_1=X_0X_1f,\ R_2=0\), the exact identity is

\[
 \mathcal A_n(RK_n)
   =-\varepsilon vX_1L_{n2}D_qR_1,\qquad
 \Theta(RK_n)=v^3(D_qf)|_\ell.
 \tag{JE8}
\]

Here \(\Theta\) is the normalized first splitting trace from (ZP35)--(ZP39).
Its target has dimension \(3q\), and it annihilates every boundary
scalar column. Thus
\(\mathcal A_n\mathscr J=\varepsilon J_1+O(\varepsilon^2)\), with
\([\mathcal A_n\mathscr B(0),J_1]\) of rank \(d_n\).
The whole vector \(\mathscr J(\varepsilon)\) is \(O(\varepsilon)\).

Choose one nonzero coefficient minor of
\([\mathcal A_n\mathscr B,\mathcal A_n\mathscr J/\varepsilon]\)
at zero. Its inverse is analytic in \(\varepsilon\). It defines one
common scalar projection for every subsequent column. For generic
nonzero \(\varepsilon\), the scalar ambient upper bound \(d_n\)
ensures that canceling these chosen coefficient coordinates cancels
the entire scalar polynomial. Hence the resulting identity holds
over the rational-function field, not merely at selected evaluations.

Two coefficient facts will be used:

* If a candidate scalar is \(O(\varepsilon)\), its coefficients on
  \(\mathscr B\) and on the **normalized** columns
  \(\mathscr J/\varepsilon\) are \(O(\varepsilon)\). Its coefficients
  on the original \(\mathscr J\) may therefore be \(O(1)\).
* For a general body selector, its constant scalar lies in the span
  of \(\mathcal A_n\mathscr B(0)\). Its normalized
  \(\mathscr J/\varepsilon\) coefficients vanish at zero. The
  coefficients on the original \(\mathscr J\) are bounded, and this
  correction has zero boundary vector.

In particular, the full scalar projection is analytic on the body
selectors and agrees at zero with their accepted boundary scalar
projection. It is incorrect to assume all original inherited
coefficients are \(O(\varepsilon)\); the argument does not do so.

## The old body projection remains regular

Before scalar projection, use the audited \(2q\) axis selectors
and \(q\) matching selectors

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathscr U_{\rm axis}
   &=\bigl(E_0,E_1,C_{2,n}^{01},
       \{C_{t,n}^{01},C_{t,n}^{11}:3\le t\le q\},
       C_{n,2}^{10}\bigr),\\
 \mathscr U_{\rm match}
   &=\bigl(C_{t,n}^{12}:1\le t\le q-1,\ C_{2,n}^{11}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{JE9}
\]

Apply the same full scalar projection to all \(3q\) columns.
For a scalar-zero row set \(f=R_1/(X_0X_1)\). The old observations
are its \(2q\) ordinary residues on \(X_2=0\) at \(L_{t0},L_{t1}\),
and its \(q\) divided matching values
\[
 b_t=\left.
       \frac{L_{t2}R_1}{X_2X_1}
      \right|_{L_{t2}=0,\ X_2=0}.
\]
All line-residue factors are canceled before the indicated matching
evaluation. The actual source conditions make these values regular.

By (ZP67)--(ZP78), their matrix on the boundary values of (JE9)
is generically invertible. This theorem already retains the scalar
axis and matching corrections; it is not a rank addition from
unrelated families.

These old observations stay analytic for small \(\varepsilon\).
In the chart \(X_1=1\), their points are
\(X_0=-u_t,-2u_t,-3u_t,\ X_2=0\). At \(\varepsilon=0\),
all three new lines there equal the nonzero number \(X_0\).
Consequently the merged new lines do not introduce a singularity
at an old observation point. The divided old matching expressions
are also analytic after their known coordinate factor is canceled.
Thus the old body matrix has an analytic inverse at zero.
For any fully scalar-corrected candidate that is \(O(\varepsilon)\),
its coefficients in this body projection are \(O(\varepsilon)\).

## Three invariant new-pole observations

Write
\(\operatorname{res}_c R=(L_{nc}R)|_{L_{nc}=0}\).
Put \(\gamma_c=G_*(c,1)\), so
\((\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_2)=(1,2,3)\). For \(c=0,1\), define

\[
 \Psi_c(R)=
 \left[
   \frac{(\operatorname{res}_c R)_1}{X_1}
   -\frac{\gamma_c\varepsilon}{v}
        \frac{(\operatorname{res}_c R)_2}{X_2}
 \right]_{X=(-\gamma_c\varepsilon,1,0)}.
 \tag{JE10}
\]

For the new state-two matching point define

\[
 \Psi_2(R)=
 \left[
   \frac{(\operatorname{res}_2 R)_1}{X_1X_2}
   -\frac{3\varepsilon}{2v}
        \frac{(\operatorname{res}_2 R)_2}{X_2^2}
 \right]_{X=(-3\varepsilon,1,0)}.
 \tag{JE11}
\]

Combine terms and cancel their common coordinate factors on the
new line **before** evaluating. For the chosen actual rows,
the state-two entry factor supplies \(X_2\), and the final
outcome-two factor supplies the further \(X_2\) in the second term.
Thus these formulas are regular as used here.

Every appended old row \(UK_n\) is killed exactly by all three
observations. Indeed its new-line residue is
\[
 U_c(X_0,\gamma_c\varepsilon X_1,G_*(c,2)vX_2).
\]
For \(c=0,1\) the two terms in (JE10) cancel identically.
For \(c=2\), the input component \(U_2\) is divisible by \(X_2\),
and the two terms in (JE11) cancel after that common factor is
removed. This applies to every inherited scalar correction,
including \(\mathscr J\), with its possibly \(O(1)\) coefficients.

On scalar-zero rows, \(\Psi_0,\Psi_1\) are nonzero row multiples
of the two new ordinary body residues: dividing additionally by
\(X_0=-\gamma_c\varepsilon\) gives the ordinary residue of \(f\).
Also \(\Psi_2\) is exactly the new divided state-two matching
observation. All these row factors are nonzero for fixed
\(\varepsilon\ne0\).

## Exact tail identities and the bounds for all projection columns

On the old \(X_2=0\) axis use \(X_1=1,\ x=X_0\).
For a nonempty old word \(Q\) with last block \(j\), let
\[
 D_Q(x)=\prod_{t\in Q}
        \frac{u_tx}{(x+u_t)(x+2u_t)}.
\]
The binary determinant and the two first-normal output entries satisfy

\[
 \left.\frac{Q_{02}-Q_{12}}{X_2}\right|_{X_2=0}
        =\frac{v_jD_Q(x)}{x}.
 \tag{JE12}
\]

To check this, the zero-sum binary row propagates through the prefix
by the product of its determinants. At the last block, the difference
of the two outcome-two coefficients divided by \(X_2\) is
\[
 \frac{v_j}{x+u_j}-\frac{v_j}{x+2u_j}
   =\frac{v_ju_j}{(x+u_j)(x+2u_j)}.
 \]
This gives (JE12), including its sign. For a word of length \(r\),
\(D_Q(x)=O(x^r)\).

For an incoming column \(C_{t,n}^{ac}\), \(c=0,1\), put
\(f=X_aK_t(a,c)\). Its term ending in \(K_n\) is killed by
\(\Psi_c\). The remaining term gives exactly

\[
 \Psi_c(C_{t,n}^{ac})
   =-\gamma_c\varepsilon\,fD_{Q^t}
       \left(1-\frac{v_{\rm last}}v\right)
       \bigg|_{x=-\gamma_c\varepsilon}.
 \tag{JE13}
\]

For \(c=0\), the surviving axis prefactor is
\(O(\varepsilon^{1+[a=0]})\); for \(c=1\) it is
\(O(\varepsilon^{[a=0]})\). A prefactor with \(a=2\) vanishes
on this ordinary axis. Incoming columns of a different divergent
state have no remaining new pole in this sector.

For divergent state two, write \(X_aK_t(a,2)=X_2\bar f\).
The corresponding exact formula is

\[
 \Psi_2(C_{t,n}^{a2})
   =-3\varepsilon\,\bar fD_{Q^t}
       \left(1-\frac{v_{\rm last}}{2v}\right)
       \bigg|_{x=-3\varepsilon}.
 \tag{JE14}
\]

The word \(Q^t\) has length \(q-1\). Formulas (JE13)--(JE14)
therefore give, on every selected incoming column, the orders
\[
 \Psi_0=O(\varepsilon^{q+1}),\qquad
 \Psi_1=O(\varepsilon^q),\qquad
 \Psi_2=O(\varepsilon^q).
 \tag{JE15}
\]
The \(E_a\) formulas have a length-\(q\) old tail and satisfy
at least these bounds. For the selected outgoing \(B_{sa}\)
columns the relevant observation either has a different new pole
or an additional vanishing \(X_2\); thus the same bounds hold.
Their \(S,T,M\) combinations preserve the bounds.

There is one further outgoing body selector, \(C_{n,2}^{10}\).
Write \(V=e_0(P_1-P_2)\). Its first two old blocks form a
commutator. On the binary axis this difference is \(O(x)\);
the common suffix of length \(q-2\) gives
\(V_1=O(x^{q-1})\). Both words end in block \(q\), and the
same last-block identity gives
\[
 V_2/X_2=-v_qV_1/x,\qquad
 \Psi_1(C_{n,2}^{10})
      =xV_1(1-v_q/v)|_{x=-2\varepsilon}
      =O(\varepsilon^q).
 \tag{JE16}
\]
Its other two invariant observations vanish.

It follows that (JE15) holds on every new scalar-basis column
in (JE7) and every raw body selector in (JE9).
It also holds after the common scalar projection of a body
selector: its new scalar-basis coefficients are bounded, and
all appended-old corrections are killed exactly by the \(\Psi\)'s.

## A coupled three-column Schur determinant

Take the actual new columns

\[
 W_0=C_{n,q}^{01},\qquad
 W_1=C_{n,q}^{11},\qquad
 W_2=C_{n,q}^{21}.
 \tag{JE17}
\]

Their entire rational rows, and their cleared scalar polynomials,
are \(O(\varepsilon)\). First apply the common full scalar
projection, then the common old body projection. Denote the
results by \(\widehat W_a\). They have scalar zero and all
\(3q\) old body observations zero.

The new scalar-basis coefficients in the first projection are
\(O(\varepsilon)\). Original inherited \(\mathscr J\)
coefficients may be \(O(1)\), but their vectors are
\(O(\varepsilon)\), and all \(\Psi\)'s kill them exactly.
Thus the scalar-corrected candidates remain \(O(\varepsilon)\).
The second projection has coefficients \(O(\varepsilon)\)
by the analytic old body inverse. Using (JE15), the total
change in their invariant observations is therefore

\[
 \Delta\Psi_0=O(\varepsilon^{q+2}),\qquad
 \Delta\Psi_1=O(\varepsilon^{q+1}),\qquad
 \Delta\Psi_2=O(\varepsilon^{q+1}).
 \tag{JE18}
\]

This is a statement about the actual two projections, not about
the ranks of three separately chosen matrices.

Put
\[
 a_t=\frac1{2u_t},\qquad b_t=\frac{v_t}{2u_t},\qquad
 \Delta a=a_q-a_{q-1},\quad \Delta b=b_q-b_{q-1}.
\]
For \(V=e_1(P_1-P_q)\), the last blocks of the two words
are \(q\) and \(q-1\), respectively. Direct expansion of the
last binary transition gives

\[
 V_1=-\Delta a\,x+O(x^2),\qquad
 V_2/X_2=\Delta b+O(x).
 \tag{JE19}
\]

Here \(q\ge4\) is more than sufficient for the preceding
state to approach the binary terminal state; in particular
there is no empty-tail exception.
The own new-line residues of (JE17), using their explicit
\(K_n(a,1)\) factors, give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Psi_0(W_0)
   &=-\varepsilon^3(\Delta a-\Delta b/v)+O(\varepsilon^4),\\
 \Psi_1(W_1)
   &=4\varepsilon^2(\Delta a-\Delta b/v)+O(\varepsilon^3),\\
 \Psi_2(W_2)
   &=9\varepsilon^2(\Delta a-\Delta b/(2v))+O(\varepsilon^3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{JE20}
\]

Every other raw sector entry is zero. For example the state-two
residue has prefactor \(3\varepsilon X_2\); after the two
divisions in (JE11) its value is
\(3\varepsilon[V_1-(3\varepsilon/(2v))(V_2/X_2)]\)
at \(x=-3\varepsilon\), giving the last line.

For \(q\ge4\), all correction orders in (JE18) are strictly
higher than the displayed own-sector leading orders. Hence

\[
 \det[\Psi_c(\widehat W_a)]_{c,a=0}^2
  =-36\varepsilon^7
       (\Delta a-\Delta b/v)^2
       (\Delta a-\Delta b/(2v))
       +O(\varepsilon^8).
 \tag{JE21}
\]

This is the determinant in the stated invariant normalizations.
It is not a valuation assertion for an unnormalized full source
matrix. The leading coefficient is a nonzero rational function:
choose \(u_q\ne u_{q-1}\), and avoid the two indicated exceptional
values of \(v\). These conditions can be intersected with the
previous scalar and body minor conditions in the same irreducible
fixed-\(G_*\) parameter family.

The \(3q\) corrected body columns and the three
\(\widehat W_a\)'s therefore have independent full old/new
ordinary-axis and matching observations, all on the same actual
scalar kernel.

## From those observations to one auxiliary polynomial map

It remains to connect this pole determinant to a **single**
\(\mathcal B_w\), rather than treating the observations as
unrelated functionals.

First fix generic old parameters and \(v\), then fix a sufficiently
small positive \(\varepsilon\ne0\) for which the scalar and pole
minors above are nonzero. All source corrections are now fixed
constant coefficients and are independent of \(w\).
For a scalar-zero corrected row, use (JE4). Evaluation at the
\(3n\) intersections of the auxiliary line with all \(L_{ta}\)
is an isomorphism on binary forms of degree \(3n-1\), provided
the nodes are distinct.

Set \(\delta=1/w\) and use representatives \(X=(-1,y,\delta)\).
The nodes are

\[
 y_{ta}(\delta)=
 \frac{1-G_*(a,2)v_t\delta}{G_*(a,1)u_t}
 \qquad(t\le n).
 \tag{JE22}
\]

At such a node, the evaluation of \(D_nf\) equals the product
of the other line values times \(\operatorname{res}_{L_{ta}}f\).
The other factors are units as \(\delta\to0\) when the axis
pole positions are pairwise distinct. For \(a=0,1\), the
limiting observation is the ordinary axis residue. For \(a=2\),
with \(b_t=(L_{t2}R_1)/(X_2X_1)\), there is the exact relation

\[
 \operatorname{res}_{L_{t2}}f=-\delta\,b_t
       \quad\hbox{on }X=(-1,y,\delta).
 \tag{JE23}
\]

Divide these \(n\) observation rows by \(\delta\) before taking
the limit. Their limits are the divided state-two matching
observations. The chart conversion from \(X_1=1\) to \(X_0=-1\)
and the extra factors between ordinary \(f\)-residues and
(JE10) are nonzero row scalings for the already fixed
\(\varepsilon\ne0\).

Thus the full old/new matrix proved nonsingular above is a
row-normalized limit of evaluations of the **same**
\(\mathcal B_w\), on the **same** scalar-zero source coefficients.
Its nonzero limiting minor implies a nonzero minor for finite
sufficiently large positive \(w\). This gives
\(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal B_w|_{\ker\mathcal A_n})=3n\).
Combined with the scalar rank \(d_n\), it proves (JE5) at length n.

The order of limits matters. The body lemmas used limits in \(v\)
only to establish nonzero rational minors; intersect their
nonvanishing opens and fix a finite \(v\) first. Next fix a
nonzero small \(\varepsilon\). Only then send \(w\) to infinity
to witness the auxiliary minor. No interchange of the
\(\varepsilon\) and \(1/w\) limits, and no scalar projection
depending on the auxiliary evaluation point, is used.

## Finite base, corroboration, and remaining scope

The accepted verifier
[source24_zero_prefix_split_jet.py](../exploration/source24_zero_prefix_split_jet.py)
and its
[saved exact results](../exploration/source24-zero-prefix-split-jet-results.json)
give the length-four base in (JE5): old scalar rank \(66\),
and inherited trace rank \(12\), computed on the same original
source coefficient kernel. Identity (JE8) identifies that trace
with a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\mathcal B_v\).
The lifting uses a nonzero minor of the joint original-source
matrix after clearing denominators. It does not lift the
individual modular kernel vectors as rational relations.
The denominator and geometry controls are recorded with the
external exact certificate. The analogous length-five trace
check gives scalar rank \(105\) and kernel trace rank \(15\).

There is also an independent finite check of the fully coupled
target in
[source24_zero_prefix_endpoint_schur.py](../exploration/source24_zero_prefix_endpoint_schur.py)
and its
[saved exact results](../exploration/source24-zero-prefix-endpoint-schur-results.json).
At the recorded prime \(1{,}000{,}003\), it uses the same prescribed
scalar basis and body projection for every column:

| Old q / new n | Scalar rank | Body rank | Three-column Schur determinant | Full auxiliary rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 / 5 | 105 | 12 | 205708 | 15 |
| 5 / 6 | 153 | 15 | 135971 | 18 |

That finite check uses the earlier endpoint candidates
\(C_{n,q}^{11},C_{n,q}^{01},C_{n,q-2}^{11}\). It corroborates the
coupled joint-rank target, but it is not a numerical verification
of (JE21), whose third candidate is \(C_{n,q}^{21}\).
The uniform proof of (JE21) is the algebra above.

Starting from the length-four exact base, the conditional theorem
therefore supplies generic full scalar/auxiliary rank at every
length \(m\ge4\) within the fixed-\(G_*\) family. For each fixed
length, the resulting nonzero rational minor can be cleared to
a polynomial; its nonvanishing also persists in the larger
common-transition parameter family. The positive parameter
region contains admissible choices because a nonzero polynomial
cannot vanish on an open positive orthant.

This conclusion is an actual zero-prefix image theorem. It does
not identify the remaining formal source-coordinate kernel with
zero, prove the separate equation-(24) independence bridge, or
supply a Lean formalization of the induction.

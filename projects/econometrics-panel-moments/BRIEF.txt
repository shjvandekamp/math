## Proof strategy and adversarial checks

Normalize one fixed effect and set the remaining η variables equal to their exponentials. Their domain is strictly positive. Expand the finite path likelihood, prove denominator positivity, and establish the exact equivalence between a moment identity for all η and a polynomial-coefficient kernel condition.

Derive the explicit 33-by-81 moment matrix and the coefficient matrix for likelihood restrictions. Thirty-three independent moments establish only dim M ≥ 33; completeness also needs the matching restriction rank of 48. Prove all rank bounds. Do not infer completeness from a numerical experiment or a crude monomial count.

Use exact rational arithmetic and symbolic algebra for discovery. Every external result used in the final theorem must become a Lean-checked identity or certificate. A nonzero minor at one admissible point can establish that a polynomial is nonzero; prove the implications for the declared parameter domain and the matching universal rank bounds separately. Do not define “generic” as “the conclusion holds.” An explicit nonzero polynomial is acceptable only with its construction, nonvanishing witness and domain/genericity argument proved. Require a fixed explicit minor from model primitives and a model-consistent witness. Prove density or the measure-zero exceptional-set statement on the economic parameter domain before calling the result generic; an open nonempty domain alone is a narrower theorem.

Have the reviewer attack the indexing, initial-state dependence, denominator clearing, parameter restrictions, exceptional sets and both directions of the kernel equivalence. A degenerate point with extra moments is not a counterexample to a generic claim. A disproof must contradict the actual quantified theorem.
